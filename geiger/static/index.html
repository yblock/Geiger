Radiation levels

The counter shows a dose rate, but health effects depend on the total dose it adds up to. Each rate is paired with the total from a year at that rate (or a few days, in the last row).

LevelDose rateTotal doseWhat it means
Safe under 1 µSv/h under 10 mSv a year Normal background is 0.05–0.3 µSv/h, about 2–3 mSv a year; higher readings are worth a look. A CT scan of the abdomen is about 10 mSv.
Concerning 1–10 µSv/h 10–100 mSv a year Fine for hours, like a flight at cruising altitude (about 6 µSv/h), but not a place to live. Radiation workers are limited to 20 mSv a year.
Harmful 10 µSv/h – 10 mSv/h 100 mSv – 1 Sv No added cancer risk has been detected below about 100 mSv; above it, lifetime risk rises by roughly 0.5% per 100 mSv. Leave and find the source.
Deadly over 10 mSv/h over 1 Sv within days Above about 0.7 Sv over a short time, radiation sickness sets in; 2.5–5 Sv kills about half of those exposed, and over 10 Sv is almost always fatal.

A rough guide for context, not safety advice. In very strong fields a Geiger tube can saturate and read low, so treat high or erratic readings as a reason to move away first. Figures from the CDC, ICRP, and Health Physics Society.

Erasing the counter's memory

Over USB the counter's memory can only be read, so erasing it happens on the counter: Main Menu → Save Data → Erase Saved Data, choose Yes, and wait a few seconds.

Counter settings

Not here: baud rate, since changing it would cut the USB connection. The clock is kept in step automatically.

Change the counter's calibration?

Calibration turns the counter's raw counts into dose rate. GQ sets it at the factory for this model's tube and everyday background gamma, and it's right for almost every use.

Changing it changes every µSv/h figure: the live reading, the alarm when it's set in µSv/h, the radiation levels comparison, dose received, and all past history shown here. The counts themselves don't change.

Only change it after measuring this counter against a reference source with a known dose rate, as described under "Calibrate the reading" in GQ's manual. A wrong value makes every dose rate wrong, usually without looking wrong.
